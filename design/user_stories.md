Who is the user?

    Primary users: Students and early-stage researchers interested in medical imaging, neuroscience, or machine learning who want to understand how MRI inputs are transformed into predicted brain stiffness maps.

    Secondary users: Instructors and researchers who want a visual way to demonstrate the pipeline or inspect model performance without having to run the entire codebase themselves.

What do they want to do with the tool? 

    Users want to explore how deep learning models can predict brain tissue stiffness maps from MRI data. They want to view MRI inputs and predicted stiffness maps, compare predictions with reference MRE maps, evaluate model performance, and understand the overall prediction process. Some users may also want to explore preprocessing techniques and investigate how the model performs on different samples.

What needs and desires do they want for the tool?

    Users want an interactive, intuitive, and educational website that makes the MRI-to-stiffness prediction process easier to understand. Key features include side-by-side image comparisons, interactive visualizations, performance metrics, prediction error maps, and step-by-step explanations of data preprocessing and model architecture. They also want clear explanations of technical concepts, sample data for experimentation, and accessible results that can be used for learning, research, or presentations. The tool should clearly communicate the model's limitations and should not present predictions as clinical diagnoses.

What is their skill level?

    Users have different levels of technical knowledge, ranging from beginners to advanced researchers. Some have little or no experience with programming, neuroscience, or medical imaging and need guided explanations. Others have intermediate Python, statistics, or data analysis skills but limited experience with MRI data or deep learning applications. Advanced users may already understand machine learning and want detailed evaluation metrics, model information, and reproducible results. Therefore, the tool should accommodate different backgrounds through a combination of beginner-friendly explanations and more technical information.
